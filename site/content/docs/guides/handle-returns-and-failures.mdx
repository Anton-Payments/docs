Both are final, and neither can be retried in place. `failed` carries a `failure_code`. `returned` carries none, because the money went out first.

<BeforeYouBegin items={["A subscription to payout.failed and payout.returned", "Your own record of each payout, keyed on its id"]} />

## Read the ending

`payout.failed` and `payout.returned` carry the payout in `data.object`, the same object `GET /v1/payouts/{id}` returns. Branch on `status`, then on `failure_code`.

<RunStep title="Read a failed payout" method="GET" path="/v1/payouts/{id}"
  pathParams={{ id: "pay_dbvqrpqukoh470i0bco0" }}
  respond={{ status: "failed", failure_code: "provider_rejected", status_message: null, failed_at: "2026-11-21T14:05:12.000Z" }}>
  For a `failed` payout, `failure_code` says what the rail reported, and `failed_at` says when. `status_message` is set only for `cannot_be_processed`.
</RunStep>

## Failed

A payout fails before it is sent, or while the rail has it. `processing` can go to `failed`, `on_hold` can go to `failed`, and so can `sent`.

| `failure_code` | Means | Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cannot_be_processed` | Anton could not process the payout and cannot say more. | Don't retry to the same payee. Contact support with the payout `id` and the `request_id`. |
| `provider_rejected` | The rail refused the payout, including when it rejects the receiving account. | Check the payee and instrument, then create a new payout. |
| `provider_unavailable` | The rail reported it was unavailable. | Create a new payout later. |
| `insufficient_funds` | The rail reported a shortfall after Anton sent the payout. Your own balance is checked earlier, at create, as `422 insufficient_balance`. | Check the amounts, then create a new payout. Contact support if it repeats. |
| `beneficiary_not_found` | Anton could not load the payee when it went to send, for example because the payee was deleted after the payout was created. | Check the payee still exists, then create a new payout. |

Treat a code you don't know as a plain failure. New values can be added without breaking you.

A failed payout cannot be revived. Fixing the cause means a new payout, with a new `Idempotency-Key`, since the old key would give you the failed payout back for 24 hours. Mark your own record as failed from the event, then decide whether to pay again.

<Warning>
  Create the replacement only when you know the first did not pay. A `failed` status is final, so it is safe. A payout that is only taking long is not.
</Warning>

## Returned

`returned` is reachable only from `sent`: the funds came back after being sent, and `payout.returned` says the receiving institution returned them. Your user did not get paid.

The whole amount comes back to your balance, fee included. The movement shows in the transactions list; [Reconcile with transactions](/guides/reconcile-with-transactions) covers reading it.

## What to tell your user

- **Failed:** the payment did not go out. Say so, and say what you will do next. For `cannot_be_processed`, don't invent a reason; Anton gives none.
- **Returned:** the payment went out and came back. Say the payee's institution sent it back, and ask for corrected details before you pay again.

Don't show `failure_code` to your user as text. It's for your code.
