A verification subject is a person or business you want checked, and its ID starts with `vsub_`. Creating one opens its first verification, `vrf_`, with a hosted link your customer completes. Anton then screens the result, and you read the outcome from the subject or from webhooks.

Verifications need the `intelligence` capability and a credential carrying the `intelligence` scope. Without it a call is `403`, with `insufficient_scope` or `capability_required`.

## Choose who delivers the link

`delivery` decides who gets the hosted link to your customer. Only `sent` proves an email went out. If the status comes back `created`, you hold the link and nobody has received it.

<Decision>
  <Choice title="Anton emails it" value='delivery: "email"' fields="email, message" set={{ delivery: "email" }} picked>
    The link goes to `email`. `message` is shown in that email, attributed to your business.
  </Choice>
  <Choice title="You deliver it" value='delivery: "manual"' fields="hosted_link" set={{ delivery: "manual" }}>
    The default when `delivery` is left out. The response carries the link and you send it through your own channel. Without an email on file, this is the only outcome.
  </Choice>
</Decision>

## Create the subject

`type` is `person` or `business`. A person completes an ID and selfie flow. A business completes its details, then a flow for each controlling owner. Only `type` and `display_name` are required.

<RunStep title="Create the subject" method="POST" path="/v1/verification-subjects"
  headers={{ "Idempotency-Key": "verify-customer-8841" }}
  body={{
    type: "person",
    display_name: "Ada Lovelace",
    email: "ada@example.com",
    external_ref: "customer_8841",
    country: "CA",
    delivery: "email",
    message: "Please verify your identity so we can activate your account.",
    link_expires_in_days: 7,
    metadata: { crm_id: "42" }
  }}>
  The subject comes back with its first verification in `verifications` and the one-time `hosted_link`.
</RunStep>

`display_name` is the legal name. It goes into the vault and comes back masked on every read. `country` is optional and worth sending, since it helps screening tell people apart. `external_ref` is your own ID, echoed back. Search matches it, or the subject ID, and never a name.

`link_expires_in_days` is `7`, `14` or `30`, and defaults to `7`. `message` is up to 500 characters, used for that one send and never stored, so it cannot be read back. `metadata` is your own string map and comes back on every read.

`hosted_link` opens a flow that collects identity documents, so treat it like a credential. It appears on the response that minted it and on no read afterwards. Replay the same request with the same `Idempotency-Key` and the link is `null`; ask for a new one with resend or regenerate.

## What happens next

`status` on the subject is the status of its current verification.

<Lifecycle states="created, sent, in_progress, processing, approved"
  notes="Link minted | Emailed by Anton | Subject opened it | Checks running | Verified"
  edges="Anton emails | Subject opens | Subject submits | Checks pass">
  Branches: `awaiting_owners` for a business whose owner flows are outstanding, `review`, `declined`, `expired` and `revoked`. `approved`, `declined`, `expired` and `revoked` are final.
</Lifecycle>

A business cannot reach `approved` until every controlling owner has. Each owner in `owners` has its own `status`, a masked name and email, and an `ownership_percentage`.

Subscribe to the `verification.link_sent`, `verification.started`, `verification.approved`, `verification.declined`, `verification.review` and `verification.expired` events, or read the subject with [`GET /v1/verification-subjects/{id}`](/reference/verifications/get-verification-subject). The read returns every verification the subject has had, newest first. List rows leave `verifications` out. Webhooks are covered in [Track payouts with webhooks](/guides/track-payouts-with-webhooks).

Every completed verification is screened, and the result is in its `screening` block with `status`, `score`, `tier`, `reasons` and an `evaluation_id`. The block is `null` until the screening has run. A result that needs a person to look at it has `status` `review`, and the verification is flagged for manual review. The concepts page has the [screening detail](/concepts/verifications).

## When a link goes wrong

Three calls cover it, and what you pick depends on what happened to the link. Each requires an `Idempotency-Key`.

| Situation | Call | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The link expired unused, or never arrived | `POST /v1/verifications/{id}/resend` | A fresh link for the same verification, re-delivered to the email on file. |
| The link may have been forwarded or exposed | `POST /v1/verifications/{id}/regenerate` | The old link and inquiry die at once. A new verification opens with `reason` `regenerated`. |
| You no longer want it to happen | `POST /v1/verifications/{id}/revoke` | The verification becomes `revoked` and its link expires. |

Resend is refused with `invalid_verification_state` on a verification that is already `approved`, `declined`, `expired` or `revoked`; open a new one instead. Revoke is refused the same way on `approved`, `declined` and `expired`, and revoking one that is already revoked returns it unchanged.

<RunStep title="Resend the link" method="POST" path="/v1/verifications/{id}/resend"
  pathParams={{ id: "vrf_01JD3XB7Q3" }}
  headers={{ "Idempotency-Key": "resend-vrf-01JD3XB7Q3-1" }}>
  A new `hosted_link` and a new `link_expires_at`. The previous link is not usable.
</RunStep>

For a business, the link that went astray may be an owner's. [`POST /v1/verifications/{id}/owners/resend`](/reference/verifications/resend-owner-link) mints a fresh link for one owner. Name the owner by exactly one of `owner_id` or `email`. An `email` must belong to an owner of that verification, or the call is `404`. With `email` you can also send `deliver: false`, and the link comes back in the response instead of in an inbox. `owner_id` always emails the address on file.

## Verify again

A subject can be verified again, for a periodic refresh or because their details changed. [`POST /v1/verification-subjects/{id}/verifications`](/reference/verifications/reverify-subject) opens a new verification with `reason` `reverification` and keeps the history. It takes an optional `metadata` map, which is stored on the new verification, and returns the new `hosted_link`. If the subject has an email on file, Anton emails it.

Rescreening is a different job. [`POST /v1/verifications/{id}/rescreen`](/reference/verifications/rescreen-verification) runs the screening again on the data Anton already holds, for example after a watchlist update. It returns the same verification with a fresh `screening` block and a new `evaluation_id`. The status does not change and nothing is re-collected. It is refused on a verification not yet screened (`created`, `sent`, `in_progress`) or one that is `revoked` or `expired`.

## What you will not get back

Owners' names and emails are masked the same way as the subject's. Reading the true name or the captured documents is a dashboard action for admins, and each access is audited. Webhook payloads carry IDs and statuses, not names, links or documents.

<Note>
  `GET /v1/verification-stats` gives your funnel in one call: totals by outcome, pass rate and median completion minutes. See [Verification overview stats](/reference/verifications/get-verification-stats).
</Note>
