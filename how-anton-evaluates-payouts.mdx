title: "How Anton Evaluates Payouts"
description: "Every payout moves through screening, velocity control, and AI risk scoring before a single dollar leaves your balance. Here's what happens — and how your integration should respond."
---

A payout created via `POST /v1/payouts` is not submitted to a rail immediately. Anton checks it first. The outcome is reflected in the payout's status: your application reads that status (and the webhook events that announce each change) and responds accordingly.

## What happens to every payout

<Steps>
  <Step title="Checks">
    The payout and its beneficiary are checked before any funds move. A payout that needs a closer look is placed `on_hold`. Anton does not say why.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your own rules">
    If your account has approval rules or velocity limits, a payout that matches one waits for a second approver (`pending_approval`) or is held.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rail submission">
    A payout that clears every check moves to `processing` and is submitted to the rail. From there `sent` and `completed` are rail-driven and reflect settlement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The payout status flow

```
created
  ├─ pending_approval ─ processing  (or cancelled)
  ├─ processing ─ sent ─ completed
  │       │         ├─ failed
  │       │         └─ returned
  │       ├─ on_hold ─ processing / cancelled / failed
  │       ├─ failed
  │       └─ cancelled
  └─ on_hold
```

`completed`, `returned`, `cancelled` and `failed` are final. Every status change emits a `payout.*` [webhook event](/api-reference/webhook-events).

## What each status means

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `created` | Accepted; checks have not started. | No action. |
| `pending_approval` | Waiting for a second person on your team to approve it, under your own approval policy. | No action. |
| `processing` | Being checked and prepared, or submitted to the rail. | Show "in flight" in your UI. |
| `on_hold` | Held for review. The payout's `status_message` is the only thing we say about it. A payout that waits much longer than usual for its checks also shows as `on_hold`. | Treat it as a normal "pending" state; do not show an error. It cannot be cancelled while held. |
| `sent` | The rail confirmed dispatch. | Show "sent" in your UI. |
| `completed` | The beneficiary received the funds. | Mark the payout done in your system. |
| `returned` | Funds came back after being sent. The source amount is credited back to your balance in the source currency and any unused FX buffer is released. The fee is retained, because the payout was attempted. | Handle the return in your accounting. |
| `failed` | The payout did not go through and will not change again. `failure_code` says what the rail reported, or is `cannot_be_processed` when we could not process the payout. We give no further detail for `cannot_be_processed`. | Review `failure_code`. Do not retry a `cannot_be_processed` payout to the same beneficiary; contact support. |
| `cancelled` | Cancelled by you before submission. | Final. |

`completed`, `returned`, `cancelled` and `failed` are final. Treat a status you do not know as in progress.

## What your integration should do

**Subscribe to webhooks. Do not poll.** The payout lifecycle is event-driven. Polling wastes rate-limit budget and misses changes.

**Treat a hold as a normal state.** Most legitimate payouts clear review quickly. When a payout is `on_hold`, show a pending state to your end user, not an error. A payout on hold cannot be cancelled until the hold ends: the cancel request returns `409 payout_on_hold`.

**Do not retry a `cannot_be_processed` payout.** Creating a new payout to the same beneficiary does not change the outcome. Contact support with the payout's `id`.

## What you can and cannot control

Checks run automatically on every payout. You cannot disable them, override them at submission, or skip them with a flag.

## What you send changes what Anton can see

Anton scores what it is given. The largest single improvement most integrations can make
is sending the `session` object on `POST /v1/payouts` — the end-user IP, timezone, and
user agent your own edge observed.

Without it the only address in the request is your server's, so network- and
location-based reasoning has nothing to work with. With it, Anton can check the payer's
country against the destination corridor, test whether the browser's timezone agrees with
where the address resolves, and tell residential traffic apart from datacenter, Tor, or
relay egress.

Every field is optional and none of them can fail a payout — invalid values come back as
`session_quality` warnings, not errors. See
[Send end-user session context](/guides/send-a-payout#send-end-user-session-context).
